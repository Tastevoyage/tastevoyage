Indian Masala Curry

A rich and aromatic curry inspired by the bold spices and comforting flavors of Indian cuisine.

Preparation 20 minutes
Cooking 35 minutes
Servings 4 people
Cuisine Indian

About Indian Masala Curry

Indian cuisine is famous for its extraordinary range of spices, cooking techniques and regional traditions. Masala refers broadly to a mixture or blend of spices used to create layers of flavor.

This recipe brings together onion, garlic, ginger, tomatoes and warm spices to create a rich curry base. It can be adapted with chicken, vegetables, paneer or other ingredients.

Ingredients

  • 500 g chicken, paneer or vegetables
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 3 tomatoes, chopped
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 1 small piece fresh ginger
  • 2 tablespoons cooking oil
  • 1 teaspoon turmeric
  • 2 teaspoons ground cumin
  • 2 teaspoons ground coriander
  • 1 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon garam masala
  • ½ cup yogurt or coconut milk
  • Fresh coriander leaves
  • Salt to taste

How To Prepare

01. Prepare the ingredients

Chop the onions and tomatoes. Crush or finely grate the garlic and ginger. Prepare the main ingredient you have selected.

02. Build the masala base

Heat oil in a pan. Add onions and cook until golden. Add garlic and ginger and cook until fragrant.

03. Add the spices

Add turmeric, cumin, coriander and chili powder. Stir gently for a short time so the spices release their aroma without burning.

04. Add tomatoes

Add chopped tomatoes and cook until they become soft and the mixture develops into a thick, flavorful masala.

05. Add the main ingredient

Add chicken, paneer or vegetables. Stir well so the masala coats everything evenly.

06. Finish the curry

Add yogurt or coconut milk and simmer gently until the curry reaches the desired consistency. Finish with garam masala and fresh coriander.

The Story Behind Indian Masala

Indian cuisine developed through centuries of regional cooking traditions. Different parts of India developed their own combinations of spices, herbs, vegetables, grains and cooking methods.

The word "masala" commonly refers to a mixture of spices. These combinations can vary greatly from one household and region to another.

This diversity is one of the reasons Indian food has become popular around the world, with countless recipes inspired by regional Indian cooking.

Chef's Tips

  • Toast spices gently to bring out their aroma, but avoid burning them.
  • Allow the onion and tomato base to cook properly for deeper flavor.
  • Adjust chili according to your preferred level of heat.
  • Fresh coriander added at the end gives the curry a bright finish.
  • Serve with rice, naan, roti or another bread of your choice.
